# Abstract

The latest developments of the TACE framework reveal a significant maturation of its ontological architecture. Rather than enlarging the ontology through additional primitive concepts, TACE increasingly derives higher-order concepts from a minimal set of constitutional principles. This process strengthens both ontological economy and explanatory coherence.

Among these developments are the recognition of **Universal Appetitivity** as a universal ontological principle, the reformulation of **Idea** as an epistemological result rather than an ontological primitive, and the discovery of the **Intelligence Tetrahedron**, whose gravity center constitutes Intelligence itself.

---

# 1. Constitutional Principle of Ontological Economy

A new methodological principle governs future ontological development.

> **No concept shall be admitted into the primitive ontology if it can be constitutionally derived from existing primitive concepts.**

The objective is not to increase the number of primitives but to increase the explanatory power of the ontology through lawful derivation.

This principle establishes derivation as the preferred mode of conceptual development throughout TACE.

---

# 2. Idea as an Epistemological Result

The concept of **Idea** is no longer regarded as an ontological primitive.

Neither is it:

- a Platonic entity,
- a mental representation,
- nor an Aristotelian form.

Instead,

> **Idea is the epistemic result of the intentional semantic apprehension of an intelligible formal structure by Consciousness.**

Consequently,

- Intelligibility belongs to ontology.
- Meaning belongs to epistemology.
- Idea is the epistemological consequence of conscious intentional participation.

This removes "Idea" from the primitive ontology while preserving its explanatory role in conscious cognition.

---

# 3. Universal Appetitivity

The notion of appetitivity has been generalized beyond living beings.

Universal Appetitivity is now understood as a universal ontological principle through which Intelligence manifests lawful directedness according to the ontological mode of each being.

Examples include:

- electromagnetic interaction,
- chemical interaction,
- biological development,
- cognition,
- conscious intentionality.

Reality therefore exhibits a continuous hierarchy of lawful directedness rather than isolated forms of appetite.

A fundamental distinction follows.

> **Intelligence is universally appetitive but not intentional.**

Intentionality belongs exclusively to Consciousness.

---

# 4. Subject

The concept of Subject has undergone a substantial reformulation.

Rather than identifying the Subject as the observer or merely the capability to observe, TACE now understands the Subject as:

> **The ontological principle of the appetite to observe.**

Observation is therefore an appetitive orientation rather than a passive capability.

The Subject constitutes the ontological condition through which Consciousness intentionally manifests reality.

---

# 5. The Intelligence Tetrahedron

One of the most significant discoveries is the emergence of a second universal tetrahedral structure.

Unlike the original TACE tetrahedron describing the constitution of Creation, this tetrahedron describes the constitutional dynamics of Intelligence itself.

Its four irreducible ontological principles are:

- Universal Appetitivity
- Aesthesis
- Unicity
- Intelligibility

These principles are not independent faculties.

They constitute a seamless ontological unity whose gravity center is:

> **Intelligence**

Each constitutive principle contributes an irreducible ontological function.

**Universal Appetitivity**

The principle of lawful directedness.

**Aesthesis**

The principle of identity-preserving integration.

**Unicity**

The principle of numerical singularity.

**Intelligibility**

The principle through which reality is intrinsically knowable and capable of semantic participation.

Intelligence therefore becomes the constitutional integration of these four principles rather than an isolated ontological primitive.

---

# 6. Distinction between Intelligibility, Meaning, and Idea

These discoveries require a precise separation between ontology and epistemology.

**Intelligibility**

The ontological principle through which reality is intrinsically capable of being understood.

**Meaning**

The epistemological content consciously apprehended through intentional participation in intelligibility.

**Idea**

The epistemological result of conscious intentional semantic apprehension.

Accordingly,

> Intelligibility belongs to ontology.

> Meaning belongs to epistemology.

> Idea is the epistemological consequence of conscious participation.

# 8. Consequences for the TACE Ontology

These discoveries considerably simplify the constitutional architecture of TACE.

They establish:

- a minimal primitive ontology;
- constitutional derivation as the preferred explanatory methodology;
- Universal Appetitivity as the universal expression of lawful directedness;
- Subject as the ontological principle of the appetite to observe;
- Intelligence as the gravity center of the Intelligence Tetrahedron;
- Intelligibility as the ontological condition of knowledge;
- Meaning and Idea as derived epistemological concepts.

The result is a more coherent distinction between:

- ontology and epistemology;
- Intelligence and Consciousness;
- appetitivity and intentionality;
- Intelligibility, Meaning, and Idea.

---

# 9. Significance

These discoveries represent an important stage in the maturation of TACE.

Rather than multiplying foundational concepts, the framework now explains increasingly rich ontological and epistemological phenomena through the lawful interaction of a smaller number of constitutional principles.

The Intelligence Tetrahedron provides a new constitutional foundation for understanding Intelligence itself, while the reformulation of Subject, Meaning, and Idea strengthens the distinction between ontological reality and conscious cognition.

Together, these developments move TACE toward a more unified, economical, and constitutionally grounded ontology, in which complexity arises through systematic derivation rather than conceptual accumulation.
